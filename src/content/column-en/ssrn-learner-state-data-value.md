## Is the value of generated data fixed?

Neural classifiers are increasingly trained with auxiliary data on top of the target-task data: texts generated by LLMs, augmented copies of real examples, and soft targets distilled from teacher models. When deciding whether to add such data, most work relies on a value attached to the data, such as the diversity of a generated set, its faithfulness to real data, or per-example contributions from Shapley values or influence estimates. These approaches treat value mainly as a property of the data.

Emotion recognition is a good place to test this assumption. LLMs can produce labeled emotional text at almost no cost. Emotion labels, however, are subjective, and annotators often disagree on the same text. The natural target of a classifier is therefore a soft distribution of human judgments.

The question of this paper is: **in emotion recognition, is the value of auxiliary data a fixed property of the data, or a relation between the data, the learner state and the evaluation objective?**

## The analysis framework

<figure>
  <img src="/column-images/ssrn-learner-state-data-value/overview-1600.webp" alt="Overview of the analysis" />
  <figcaption><strong>Figure 1.</strong> Flow of the analysis (Figure 1 of the paper). The inputs are a real pool with human label distributions, a set of auxiliary texts, and a student trained on n real examples. Stages (a)–(d) vary each condition of the value V(Q | P, θ<sub>n</sub>, L) in turn.</figcaption>
</figure>

The central quantity is the **real-data exchange rate** f(n). One student is trained on n real examples, and another on the same n examples plus an auxiliary pool of m examples, with the same recipe and seed. Both are evaluated by cross-entropy against the human label distribution, after temperature scaling. The difference is divided by the risk reduction that m additional real examples would give at the same n. A value f(n) = 0.5 means the pool is worth 0.5m real examples; a negative value means the pool increases the risk.

The analysis has four stages.

1. **Vary the learner state**: change the real-data size n from 500 to the full pool and trace f(n) of the same auxiliary pool along the learning curve.
2. **Vary the label direction**: keep the auxiliary texts and their argmax labels fixed, match the mean entropy, and change only where the soft probability mass goes.
3. **Vary the text source**: apply the same label directions to generated texts and to held-out real texts.
4. **Test whether value can be predicted**: check whether quantities computed before mixed training rank the pools in the same order as the measured value.

<figure>
  <img src="/column-images/ssrn-learner-state-data-value/directions-1600.webp" alt="Four label directions" />
  <figcaption><strong>Figure 2.</strong> The four label directions (Figure 2 of the paper). Black is the mass on the original label y; grey is the moved mass. Teacher uses the probabilities of a classifier trained on real data, uniform corresponds to label smoothing, prior uses the class prior of the real pool, and permuted applies a fixed class derangement to the teacher probabilities. All share the same mean entropy H.</figcaption>
</figure>

As a control for stage 2, the paper also uses a **teacher trained on exactly the same n real examples as the student**. With a full-pool teacher, a benefit of the teacher direction could simply come from distilling real data that the student has not seen.

## Experiment 1: does the value of the same data change with the real-data size?

<p class="experiment-label">Experiment 1</p>

<div class="setup">

**Setup**

- Data: GoEmotions (English Reddit comments, 28 classes, target = mean of about 3.4 annotators' votes) and WRIME (Japanese social-media posts, three readers' votes, K = 9)
- Auxiliary pools: for GoEmotions, three generation methods with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (label-conditioned generation, AugGPT, CoTAM) and five methods that edit seed texts (EDA, AEDA, back-translation, LLM paraphrasing, embedding-based oversampling); for WRIME, posts generated by Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and 4,500 held-out real posts. m = 4,500 per pool
- Students: RoBERTa-base (3 seeds) and DeBERTa-v3-base (1 seed) on GoEmotions; BERT-base Japanese v3 (3 seeds) on WRIME
- Real-data grid: n ∈ {500, 1k, 2k, 4k, 8k, 16k, full}; the late stage is the three largest sizes (8k, 16k, full)

</div>

<figure>
  <img src="/column-images/ssrn-learner-state-data-value/exchange-curve-1600.webp" alt="Exchange rate across real-data sizes" />
  <figcaption><strong>Figure 3.</strong> Exchange rate of label-conditioned generated data with one-hot labels, GoEmotions, RoBERTa-base. The four points are those reported in the paper's text. The risk change crosses zero at n<sub>0</sub> ≈ 6,700.</figcaption>
</figure>

The exchange rate of the same generated pool changed with the real-data size, and even changed sign. It was 0.00 at n = 500 and 0.23 at 4k, then −0.75 at 16k and −0.71 on the full pool. AugGPT and CoTAM also rose before crossing zero, and f(500) > f(full) held for all three LLM-based methods in all four runs.

<figure>
  <img src="/column-images/ssrn-learner-state-data-value/pool-rates-1600.webp" alt="Late-stage exchange rates of different pools" />
  <figcaption><strong>Figure 4.</strong> Exchange rates at the late stage. Left: GoEmotions, full pool. Right: WRIME, late-stage mean. Generated texts with one-hot labels go negative; teacher soft labels and real texts stay positive.</figcaption>
</figure>

The labels also changed the curve. When a full-pool teacher relabeled the same generated texts with its argmax labels, the rate on the full pool was still −0.51. When the teacher's probabilities were used as soft labels, the rate stayed positive at every size and was 0.31 on the full pool. Back-translation, which keeps real texts with small edits, stayed at 0.57 on the full pool. On WRIME, generated texts with one-hot labels stayed near zero up to 8k and then fell to a late-stage rate of −0.65, whereas real texts kept +0.67 with reader labels and +0.70 with writer labels.

The sign did not depend on the estimator. A derivative-free equivalent-size rate f<sub>eq</sub> agreed in sign in all 256 uncensored cells of GoEmotions and all 76 of WRIME. The zero point did depend on the student: with DeBERTa-v3-base, label-conditioned generation crossed zero at about 14,700 real examples, much later than with RoBERTa-base.

## Experiment 2: changing only the direction of the soft labels

<p class="experiment-label">Experiment 2</p>

The text, the argmax label and the mean entropy were fixed, and only the direction of the soft target was changed, at two mixing levels a = 0.25 and 0.5.

<figure>
  <img src="/column-images/ssrn-learner-state-data-value/direction-1600.webp" alt="Entropy-matched direction intervention" />
  <figcaption><strong>Figure 5.</strong> Effect of entropy-matched label directions (Table 2 of the paper). Left: WRIME late-stage exchange rate (mean of 3 seeds, higher is better). Right: GoEmotions late-stage change in calibrated cross-entropy at a = 0.5 for two seeds (lower is better). The band and dashed line mark one-hot labels.</figcaption>
</figure>

On GoEmotions, the teacher direction at a = 0.5 lowered the late cross-entropy in both seeds (−0.0213 and −0.0176), whereas one-hot labels raised it (+0.0174 and +0.0219). The uniform direction kept most of the harm, and the permuted direction was worse than one-hot. On WRIME, the teacher direction at a = 0.5 reached +0.20, while uniform, prior and permuted ranged from −0.55 to −0.93. At a = 0.25 the teacher direction gave −0.07, reducing but not removing the harm. Both the direction and the amount of moved mass mattered.

To check whether this is just distillation, the teacher was trained on the same real data as the student.

<figure>
  <img src="/column-images/ssrn-learner-state-data-value/same-teacher-1600.webp" alt="Same-data teacher on WRIME" />
  <figcaption><strong>Figure 6.</strong> Exchange rates of generated texts on WRIME when the teacher is trained on exactly the student's n real examples (Table 3 of the paper).</figcaption>
</figure>

At n = 8k the teacher direction reached +0.48, close to the +0.47 obtained with the full-pool teacher, while uniform and permuted gave −0.24 and −0.18. Extra real data known to the teacher cannot explain this. On the full pool, however, the teacher direction was only +0.02, and every other pool stayed below −1.1. The teacher direction removed the harm at a = 0.5, but it did not turn generated data into a benefit at every n.

## Experiment 3: does a wrong label cost the same on generated and real texts?

<p class="experiment-label">Experiment 3</p>

A two-by-two design crossed the text source (generated or real) with the direction (teacher or permuted). The drop in the late-stage rate under the permuted direction is the cost of a wrong direction, written Δ<sub>gen</sub> for generated texts and Δ<sub>real</sub> for real texts.

On WRIME, Δ<sub>gen</sub> = −0.97 and Δ<sub>real</sub> = −0.37, and real texts with permuted labels kept a positive rate of +0.38. On GoEmotions, Δ<sub>gen</sub> = −1.07 and Δ<sub>real</sub> = −0.88, and real texts with permuted labels had a negative rate of −0.29. Δ<sub>gen</sub> < Δ<sub>real</sub> held in each of the three seeds on both corpora. Real texts reduced the cost of a wrong direction but did not always protect against it.

Matching the entropy does not match the size of the perturbation across sources. On WRIME, the mean total variation (TV) between teacher and permuted targets was 0.31–0.32 on generated texts and 0.26–0.27 on real texts. The generated-text perturbation was therefore weakened until its TV or its KL divergence equaled that of the real texts.

<figure>
  <img src="/column-images/ssrn-learner-state-data-value/matched-cost-1600.webp" alt="Strength-matched cost of the permuted direction" />
  <figcaption><strong>Figure 7.</strong> Cost of the permuted direction with perturbation strength matched between text sources (Table 6 of the paper). Parentheses count the seeds with Δ<sub>gen</sub> < Δ<sub>real</sub>.</figcaption>
</figure>

On WRIME, TV matching moved Δ<sub>gen</sub> from −0.97 to −0.73, still about twice Δ<sub>real</sub>; the difference in strength explained about 40% of the gap. On GoEmotions the gap was small from the start. It kept its direction in the seed means, but under TV matching one of the three seeds gave −0.88 against −0.89. The larger cost on generated texts thus remained clearly on WRIME and weakly on GoEmotions.

## Experiment 4: can value be predicted before mixed training?

<p class="experiment-label">Experiment 4</p>

The paper tested whether quantities available before mixed training rank the pools in the same order as their exchange rates, using Spearman ρ (positive = agreement with the value ranking).

| Predictor | Setting | Seeds | ρ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uncalibrated e<sub>mis</sub> (static) | GoEmotions, 9 pools | 0, 1 | 0.33, 0.30 |
| Uncalibrated β<sub>KL</sub> (static) | GoEmotions, 9 pools | 0, 1 | 0.88, 0.80 |
| Gradient cosine | WRIME, 7 pools × 3 n | 0 | −0.61 |
| Gradient inner product | WRIME, 7 pools × 3 n | 0 | −0.89 |
| Gradient cosine | WRIME, 7 pools × 3 n | 1, 2 | −0.32 |
| Gradient inner product | WRIME, 7 pools × 3 n | 1, 2 | −0.89 |
| Calibrated gradient cosine | WRIME, 7 pools × 3 n | 1, 2 | +0.20 |
| Calibrated gradient inner product | WRIME, 7 pools × 3 n | 1, 2 | −0.58 |
| Learner–label disagreement D | WRIME, 7 pools × 3 n | 1, 2 | 0.69 |

Among the static scores, β<sub>KL</sub> tracked the direction reasonably well, but it does not depend on the learner state, so it cannot follow the sign change over n seen in Experiment 1. e<sub>mis</sub> counted the low-confidence permuted direction as a small error and missed the most harmful pool.

First-order gradient alignment pointed the wrong way. The cosine between the development-set gradient and the auxiliary-pool gradient was positive in all 21 combinations (0.37 to 0.56), so to first order every pool should lower the risk. Yet the pools with the largest inner products were the most harmful. With the calibrated objective, the mean correlation of the cosine rose to +0.20, but it varied from −0.89 to +0.82 across seeds and n. The disagreement D failed in the same two ways in both seeds: for example, the uniform direction had a small D but caused the largest late harm.

Running **a few actual updates** on the auxiliary pool alone revealed the ranking quickly.

<figure>
  <img src="/column-images/ssrn-learner-state-data-value/trajectory-1600.webp" alt="Update-trajectory probe" />
  <figcaption><strong>Figure 8.</strong> Rank correlation between the change in test cross-entropy −ΔCE(t) and the exchange rate, when WRIME students at n = 16k are updated on each auxiliary pool alone. Values as reported in the paper's text.</figcaption>
</figure>

The rank correlation was 0.63 after one update, 0.90 after two and 0.93 after five, and it stayed between 0.88 and 0.94 until the end of three epochs (423 steps). None of the 19 harmful cells first lowered the risk and later raised it. However, restarting from a converged student raised the risk at the first step for every pool, including real examples the student had already seen, so this protocol cannot observe a benefit of the first step. The probe also used the test set, so it is not yet a selection procedure.

## Experiment 5: does the sign depend on the metric?

<p class="experiment-label">Experiment 5</p>

For the pools of the two-by-two design, the paper also reports the late-stage change in the Brier score and in macro-F1.

| Corpus | Pool | ΔBrier ↓ | ΔMacro-F1 ↑ |
|---|---|---|---|
| GoEmotions | Gen / teacher | −0.0016 | +0.020 |
| GoEmotions | Gen / permuted | +0.0029 | +0.017 |
| GoEmotions | Real / teacher | −0.0049 | +0.026 |
| GoEmotions | Real / permuted | −0.0043 | +0.032 |
| WRIME | Gen / teacher | −0.0030 | +0.021 |
| WRIME | Gen / permuted | +0.0041 | +0.019 |
| WRIME | Real / teacher | −0.0061 | +0.014 |
| WRIME | Real / permuted | −0.0058 | +0.008 |

On GoEmotions, real texts with permuted labels had a negative exchange rate of −0.29 under cross-entropy but reduced the Brier score by 0.0043. In the other seven pools, the signs of cross-entropy and Brier agreed. Macro-F1 improved for all eight pools, including generated texts with permuted labels, which were harmful under both cross-entropy and Brier. A pool that improves the predicted class can still harm the probabilistic fit to human judgments.

## What we found and what remains open

The findings are:

- The same generated data helped learners with little real data and harmed learners with much, and the zero point depended on the student model.
- At fixed entropy, the direction of soft labels changed the value. Only the teacher direction removed the harm, and a same-data teacher ruled out a pure distillation account.
- A wrong label direction cost more on generated texts than on real texts, clearly on WRIME and weakly on GoEmotions.
- No static or first-order quantity ranked the harm, whereas a few actual updates did, and the sign of the value could differ between metrics.

To explain the dependence on the learner state, the paper proposes a hypothesis: the value of an auxiliary example may depend on how much information it adds that the current learner has not yet captured. The information content of the texts was not measured, so this remains a hypothesis. The same holds for the explanation of why real texts stayed useful under wrong labels (they may contain causes, targets and context that let a learner recover the emotion).

There are limitations. Both corpora are emotion corpora, so the results do not show that the relational view holds for neural learning in general, and there is one main student per corpus and a small number of generators. The permuted direction preserved the argmax label, and the perturbation strength was not matched across corpora. The exchange rate is coarse at large n (on GoEmotions a 0.001 change in cross-entropy corresponds to 0.06–0.08 in f), so the claims rest on signs and rankings, and the pool size m was not varied. The target distribution is estimated from a few annotators. The GoEmotions direction intervention used a full-pool teacher with two seeds, and the gradient probes used the development set whereas the exchange rate used the test set.

Future work includes varying the size of the auxiliary pool, comparing corpora at matched perturbation strengths, and separating curvature effects from mini-batch noise in the onset of harm. The author also plans to measure how much information generated and real texts carry for emotion inference, to develop generators that reproduce the joint structure of texts and human judgment distributions, and to test state dependence beyond emotion recognition.
